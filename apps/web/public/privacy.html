Privacy

Last updated 10 October 2026

zoom3d is a small hobby project: a retro 3D room where friends meet as their camera faces. This page says what happens to your data when you use it.

Your camera and microphone

Video and sound go directly between the people in your room (peer to peer, over WebRTC). They do not pass through our server and are never recorded.

What the server keeps

While a room exists, the server holds what the others in it need to see: your display name, your picture if you chose one, where you stand and what you hold. A room is forgotten a short while after everyone has left. During a server restart it is written to disk briefly and deleted again on start.

Server logs record technical events, like a connection opening or a room closing, with random ids and a scrambled form of the room link. They never contain names, pictures or room links. Like most websites, our web servers may also keep standard access logs, including IP addresses, for operation and security.

What stays in your browser

Your name, picture, camera and microphone choices, volume and drink are remembered in your browser (local storage), so you don't have to set them again. They stay on your device, except your name and picture, which the people in your room see.

Usage statistics

To learn how zoom3d is used (for example, whether people find each other, or how often connections drop), the app sends anonymous events to Amplitude. The events use a random id that is forgotten when you reload or close the page. Nothing is stored on your device for this: no cookies, no local storage. No IP address, name or room link is included.

Error reports

When something breaks, technical details (the error, your browser and the app version) go to Sentry so we can fix it. Room links are removed first.

Why, and your rights

We process this data because we have a legitimate interest in keeping zoom3d working and making it better. Amplitude and Sentry may process it in the United States. We don't sell data or use it for advertising. Questions or requests: open an issue on GitHub.
